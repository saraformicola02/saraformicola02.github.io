Multitemporal MNDWI Analysis of Landsat 8 Data in Google Earth Engine for Monitoring Shoreline Retreat at Lake Albano (Castelli Romani, 2015-2025)

The theoretical premise concerns water indices. The NDWI combines NIR and SWIR to detect water in vegetation and on surfaces, while the MNDWI variant replaces NIR with the green band, separating open water, vegetation and built-up areas more effectively. The case study is Lake Albano, the deepest volcanic lake in Italy, whose level has been falling progressively since the 1980s, by about 7 meters in total and about 20 cm per year at present, with an estimated loss of more than 40 million cubic meters of water. The main causes are the intensive withdrawals of the Sforza Cesarini well, which supplies eleven municipalities, and the sealing of soils through urbanization, which hinders aquifer recharge. The analysis used a Landsat 8 collection filtered between June 1 and September 30, 2025. The MNDWI was computed with bands B3 (green) and B6 (SWIR1) on the median composite, using the standard threshold MNDWI > 0 to identify water. The overall map clearly highlights the two volcanic lakes of the area, Albano and the smaller Nemi, while the rest of the territory, whether urban, agricultural or forested, shows low values. The core of the work is a ten-year comparison between 2015 and 2025 on Lake Albano alone, obtained by extracting and overlaying the lake outlines of the two years. The perimeters almost completely coincide along the southern and eastern shores, which are steep and rocky. Along the northern shore, near Via Spiaggia del Lago, the 2025 contour is set back relative to the 2015 one. This is the morphologically flattest stretch, where even small changes in level produce a visible retreat. The result is consistent with the decline documented by hydrometric measurements and confirms the usefulness of the index for monitoring long-term hydrological processes.
