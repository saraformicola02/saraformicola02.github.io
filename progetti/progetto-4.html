Unsupervised Classification with the K-means Algorithm of Landsat 7 Imagery in Google Earth Engine for Land Cover Mapping of the Island of Mallorca (Spain, 2020)

Machine learning allows a system to learn from data without being explicitly programmed, and in remote sensing it makes it possible to handle high-dimensional imagery and complex spectral signatures. In unsupervised learning no labels are provided: the algorithm searches for structure in the data and groups pixels according to their spectral similarity. The output is a set of clusters that carry no meaning in themselves, so the operator must interpret them afterwards, using prior knowledge or reference data, and assign them to land cover categories. This project applies the approach to the island of Mallorca, Spain, with the goal of automatically classify its main land cover types into four classes. The method chosen is K-means, the most widely used clustering algorithm in remote sensing. Given a number K of clusters, it places initial centroids in feature space, assigns each pixel to the nearest one, recomputes each centroid as the mean of its assigned pixels, and repeats these last two steps until the assignments no longer change significantly. The analysis was carried out in Google Earth Engine on Landsat 7 Level 2, Collection 2, Tier 1 imagery acquired between August 1 and December 31, 2020, excluding scenes with more than 50% cloud cover. From the median composite, a random sample of 5,000 pixels was extracted within the area of interest at the native Landsat resolution of 30 meters. These pixels and their multispectral signatures formed the training set on which the clusterer was fitted. The trained model was then applied to the entire image, assigning every pixel to one of the four classes. The interpretation of the clusters rests on their geographic distribution. Brown corresponds to agricultural areas, pink to urban surfaces and bare soil, yellow to natural vegetation, and green to water and the surrounding sea. The final map is consistent with the known morphology and land use of the island, which shows that K-means can provide a reliable first mapping of land cover even without reference data or training labels.
